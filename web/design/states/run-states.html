Run states

Four states, from core/run.go:13. Every chip carries an icon, a word and a colour — all three, always. Colour alone fails a colour-blind reader and fails again at low contrast; in a product whose only job is to report what happened truthfully, that is not a cosmetic defect.

The four

Running The pod exists. Written on a fact the control plane observes — never on the pod's word. Succeeded The playbook ran to completion and reported success. Failed The playbook itself failed. A verdict about your code. Interrupted The run was cut short. Not a verdict about the playbook — which is why it is slate and not red. Never shown without its cause.

Interrupted always carries its cause

Interrupteduser_interrupt Someone saw something wrong and stopped it. strong signal
Interruptedadmin_killed Stopped by an administrator. Still owes a who and a why. deliberate
Interruptedserver_task_timeout Configuration, or a host that stopped answering. investigate
Interruptedpod_oom_killed Resources. Raise the limit, or shrink the work. resources
Interruptedpod_evicted Infrastructure. Nothing to do with the playbook. infra
Interruptednode_lost Infrastructure. Nothing to do with the playbook. infra
Interruptedclient_disconnect_timeout Accidental. Signals nothing — a laptop closed, no more. noise
Interruptedunknown An honest admission, kept on purpose. Shown as such — never dressed up as a plausible cause. unknown

The mistake this component exists to prevent

✗ Never
Interrupted
A bare interrupted. The reader cannot tell a colleague hitting Ctrl-C because they saw a mistake from a laptop that went to sleep. Those two demand opposite responses, and the badge gives no way to choose.
✓ Always
Interrupteduser_interrupt
State and cause are one component, not a chip plus an optional detail somewhere below the fold. If the cause cannot be rendered, the state must not be rendered either.
Where this rule comes from. Not from a style guide — from core/run.go:19, which says it in as many words: "interrupted without the cause is almost useless: a user's Ctrl-C is a strong signal — they saw something wrong — where a client_disconnect_timeout is accidental and signals nothing. Confusing them amounts to throwing away the information that matters."

The API enforces the same shape: a Closure is refused if it carries interrupted without a valid cause, and refused again if it carries a cause on any other state. The UI mirrors an invariant that already exists — it does not invent one.
